Understanding ISA-95 Standard

The international standard for developing automated interfaces between enterprise and control systems. Master the foundation that shapes modern MES implementations — through the lens of an oral-solid-dose (tablet) plant.

🎯
What is ISA-95?
ANSI/ISA-95 (IEC 62264)
📋
An international standard defining how enterprise systems (ERP) integrate with manufacturing control systems (MES/MOM)
🏭
Provides consistent terminology and models for describing manufacturing operations across all industries
🔗
Creates a "common language" so different software systems from different vendors can work together
📊
Defines standardized data models for Equipment, Materials, Personnel, Operations, and Segments
💡
Why Does It Matter?
Real-world business value
💊
Pharma Example: When SAP sends production order PO-77231 for 400 cases (1.6 M tablets) of Cardexil 10 mg, MES knows exactly what "Work Center" (Line 3), "Material" (CDX-10, batch B-CX-2417) and "Operation" (Blistering) mean — no translation needed
⚙️
Integration: Reduces custom integration code by 60-70% using standard interfaces like B2MML
🔄
Flexibility: Swap ERP or MES vendors without rebuilding all integrations from scratch
📈
Scalability: Add new plants or production lines following the same standard structure
🔥

💊 Pharma Traceability Example
EU FMD Unique-Identifier Recall Scenario:
A pharmacy scans a Cardexil 10 mg carton at dispense and reports a leaking blister pocket. The carton's DataMatrix UI (GTIN 00359123000108 · SN 4T7K9DQ2CX41 · batch B-CX-2417 · exp 2028-06) travels back through the national verification system and the EU Hub to Helvion's L3 serialization manager HP2-SER.

Without ISA-95: Recall the whole batch — 400 cases, 80,000 cartons, 1.6 M tablets — "just in case"
With Material Model: Serial → blister lot → lidding-foil lot FL-2216, reel 4 (a bad splice) → the 22 minutes it ran on BL3 → 22 min × 400 blisters/min ÷ 2 blisters/carton = 4,400 carton UIs (22 cases) — one serial range, pulled through the EU Hub
Result: 378 of 400 cases stay released; the recall is a serial-range decommission, not a market-wide withdrawal

WHO performs the work

Manages worker qualifications, certifications, and role assignments. Critical for compliance (GMP, EU Annex 11 / 21 CFR Part 11 e-signatures, potent-product handling).

🔄 ERP Integration Flow
1. SAP sends Production Order PO-77231 (Operations Schedule); Cadence APS releases the sequence — the MES never re-sequences
2. MES converts to Work Order with Segments (compression → coating → blistering → cartoning/serialization → aggregation)
3. Shop floor executes operations; every step lands in the eBR
4. MES sends confirmations back (Operations Response) — and the batch is only sellable once QP certificate QP-2417 is signed and the FMD upload is acknowledged

Physical Asset Model
Actual physical machines and devices

Represents the actual machines - distinct from the Equipment Model which is role-based.

🔑
Key Distinction:
Equipment = Role/Function ("BL3")
Physical Asset = Actual machine (blister machine Serial# BLM-2019-3316)

Why Separate Models?

Equipment View

Production sees: "BL3"

Focus: What function it performs

Physical Asset View

Maintenance sees: Blister machine SN# BLM-2019-3316

Focus: Service history, spare parts

🔄 Blister Machine Replacement Scenario
When the blister machine is replaced after a major rebuild, the Equipment ("BL3") stays the same in MES, but the Physical Asset changes from SN# BLM-2019-3316 to SN# BLM-2025-0142. Recipes, OEE history and the Sepasoft path stay put; the new asset goes through change control and IQ/OQ re-qualification before it may run a GMP batch — and the eBR records which physical asset produced every lot.

ISA-95 Segments

Understanding Process Segments, Product Segments, and how they define manufacturing operations

⚠️
Critical Concept: Segments are fundamental building blocks in ISA-95 that define HOW operations are performed. They're essential for recipe management, scheduling, and performance tracking.
🔄
Process Segment
The "HOW" of manufacturing - Independent of product

A Process Segment defines a step or phase of production that is independent of any specific product. It describes the capabilities and resources needed.

🏭
Equipment Requirements: What equipment is needed (Blister Machine, Cartoner + Serialization Station)
👷
Personnel Requirements: Skills/certifications needed (Blister Cert, Potent-Product Handling)
📦
Material Requirements: Types of materials consumed/produced
⏱️
Duration: Expected time to complete the segment
💊 Pharma Process Segment Example
Process Segment: "Blistering"

This segment defines the general capability to form, fill and seal blisters — it is NOT tied to any specific product or strength.

• Equipment: Any Blister Machine-class unit with fill/empty-pocket camera (BL3 or BL5)
• Personnel: Operator with Blister Machine Certification
• Materials: Bulk coated tablets + forming film + lidding foil (input), Blisters (output)
• Rate: up to 400 blisters per minute

Process Segment Structure

Process Segment: "Fluid-Bed Drying" (the ISA-88 side)

EQUIPMENT SPEC

Fluid-Bed Dryer + inline NIR moisture probe (FBD2)

PERSONNEL SPEC

Granulation Operator + e-Sig Authority

MATERIAL IN

Wet Granulate (granulation batch GB-)

MATERIAL OUT

Dried Granulate

Parameters: Inlet air 60–70 °C, LOD target 2.0 % (window 1.5–2.5 %), endpoint by inline NIR — audited hours later by the QC lab's LOD oven / Karl Fischer

Sepasoft Recipe Manager

Process Segments map to Recipe Phases in Sepasoft. Each phase defines equipment, parameters, and expected durations.

📦
Product Segment
Product-specific version of a Process Segment

A Product Segment is the product-specific implementation of a Process Segment. It defines exactly how to make a specific product using the general process capability.

💡
Think of it this way:
Process Segment = "I can blister tablets" (capability)
Product Segment = "Blister Cardexil 10 mg with these exact parameters"

Process vs Product Segment

Aspect Process Segment Product Segment
Scope Generic capability Product-specific
Example "Blistering" "Cardexil 10 mg Blistering Op 20"
Parameters Ranges/defaults Exact values
Materials Material classes Specific SKUs / batch numbers
Reusability High (many products) Low (one product)
💊 Product Segment Example
Product: Cardexil 10 mg film-coated tablet (CDX-10)
Product Segment: "Cardexil 10 mg Blistering"

Based on: Process Segment "Blistering"
Specific Material: Coated tablets of batch B-CX-2417 + PVC/PVDC forming film + Al lidding foil (lot FL-2216)
Format: 10 tablets per blister, 2 blisters per carton (not a range)
Seal Temperature: 180 °C exactly
Master Recipe: R-CDX-07 v12

🛢️
BULK-TABLET HOLD — where batch ends and discrete begins. Upstream runs as ISA-88 batches (recipe R-CDX-07 v12, phases, an electronic batch record, kilograms and LOD %); bulk coated tablets of batch B-CX-2417 wait in drums until packaging release. From BL3 onward everything is countable — blisters, cartons, bundles, cases, one pallet SSCC — and every carton carries its own FMD identity. One MES must speak both languages: the batch record upstream, OEE and serialization downstream.
Segment Dependency Types
⏭️
Sequential: Must complete in order (Granulation → Drying → Blending)
⏸️
Parallel: Can run simultaneously (Coating-suspension prep in CK1 + Compression on PR3)
🔀
Alternative: Choose one path (Route Cardexil to Press 3 OR Press 5 — but Zentrix 50 mg never leaves segregated Line 5)
🔁
Loop: Repeat until condition (LOD outside 1.5–2.5 % → keep drying)
